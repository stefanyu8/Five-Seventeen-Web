The brand is tested before anyone speaks.

A guest decides how they feel about a place in the first few seconds, and none of those seconds involve reading a brand book. They involve a sign they either found or missed, a menu that either answered the question or made them ask, and a room that either matched what they were promised or quietly did not.

Five-Seventeen designs for those seconds. Every decision on a project has to survive them, which is why the work is judged in the room and not on a screen.

Who the studio works with

Three sectors, chosen because they share the same problem: the product is experienced in a physical space, and the brand has to do its work before the person gets there.

  • HospitalityRestaurants, bars, cafés, boutique hotels, and venues.
  • WellnessYoga, pilates, barre, gyms, and studios built around a practice.
  • Med spa and aestheticsClinics and practices where presentation reads as clinical credibility.
  • Everything elseOutside these three, the studio is not the right choice and will say so.

The process

Five to six weeks, four phases, and a small number of decisions made in a fixed order. Nothing moves forward until the phase before it is settled, because a system built on an unresolved question falls apart at the first print run.

  1. ContextWhat the business actually sells, who walks in, and what they compare you to before they choose. This phase ends with a written position you can argue out loud.
  2. DirectionTwo routes, developed far enough to be judged fairly. You keep one. The other is closed, not saved for later, because a brand with a second opinion attached never settles.
  3. BuildThe chosen route becomes a system: marks at every scale, color that holds in print and on screen, type that reads at arm's length and at forty feet. Then the assets the business needs on day one.
  4. HandoverGuidelines written to be used, plus an hour with your team. The measure of this phase is whether a printer, a signmaker, or a new hire can work from it without calling us.

How the studio operates

  • One package, no pieces. Buying design in fragments is what produces a room that says four things at once.
  • Two directions, then one. Options are for choosing, not for collecting. The unchosen route is closed the day the decision is made.
  • Decisions over deliverables. A long file list is easy to produce. A brand that survives a menu reprint is not.
  • The room is the test. Nothing is approved on a screen alone. If it does not hold at the size and distance a guest sees it, it is not finished.
  • No brand by committee. One person decides. Consensus produces the average of every opinion in the room, and average is what you are trying to leave.
  • The work is done in English and Spanish. Either language, whichever the business runs on.
